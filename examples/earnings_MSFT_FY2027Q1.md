## What drove the quarter (MD&A)

- Revenue rose 18.8% to $92.28B from $77.67B, with net income up 32.0% and diluted EPS up 32.3% to $4.92 [^9][^10][^11].
- The Agents and Infra segment led, with revenue up 25%, and Azure revenue rose $10.8 billion or 48% on demand across the platform [^12].
- Microsoft 365 cloud revenue increased 17%, driven by higher revenue per user from upsell to Microsoft 365 Copilot, E5 and E7, and by growth in GitHub Copilot usage [^12].
- Gross margin fell to 67.8% (-1.3%) as segment cost of revenue rose 35%, yet operating margin still improved to 49.8% (up 0.9%) [^13][^14][^12].
- Devices and Consumer revenue fell 3%, as Windows OEM and devices revenue decreased 22% on the prior-year Windows 10 end-of-support benefit, OEM inventory drawdowns and weaker PC demand [^12][^15].

## What changed in risk factors

18 paragraph(s) in the 10-Q's Part II, Item 1A have no close match in the latest 10-K's Item 1A; the summary covers the first 8.

- The cybersecurity risk factor now says AI use, including autonomous or semi-autonomous agents, may create new attack surfaces, so AI and cybersecurity risks "increasingly overlap and amplify one another." [^16]
- It adds that AI agents' actions can be hard to predict, attribute, contain or audit, and that interactions among agents may produce or amplify unexpected or malicious behavior. [^16]
- It adds that AI models and agents with access to internal infrastructure, sensitive data or development environments may present risks like highly privileged users or threat actors, and that third parties face similar AI-enhanced risks. [^17]
- It states that the advancement and spread of AI models is accelerating vulnerability discovery, so Microsoft must speed up its development and software-update processes. [^18]
- The AI risk factor now stresses that agentic systems may pursue unintended goals or be manipulated through prompts or compromised tools to disclose information, conceal activity or take unauthorized actions. [^19]
- The passage on protecting information in products now refers to third parties scraping content using "bots, agents, or other measures." [^20]
